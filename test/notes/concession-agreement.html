Reading a concession agreement

24 July 2026 · 6 min

A concession agreement is a long document written to be unreadable in order, and perfectly readable out of order. I give students the same four-pass method I use myself, and it turns a 300-page contract into an afternoon.

Start at the payment mechanism

Everything else in the contract exists to protect the payment mechanism. Find it (usually a schedule, rarely the main body) and work out three things: what triggers payment, what reduces it, and who measures the thing being paid for. If availability is measured by the operator, you have found the project's central risk before reading a word of the risk allocation clauses.

Whoever holds the measuring instrument holds the contract.

Then the termination table

Termination payments tell you what the parties actually believe the asset is worth, and which failures they expect. Compare compensation on authority default, contractor default, and force majeure. A wide gap between the first two is normal; a narrow one means somebody negotiated well.

Third pass: the definitions

Only now read the definitions, and only the ones the first two passes made important. "Relief Event", "Compensation Event" and "Qualifying Change in Law" carry most of the money.

pdftotext -layout concession.pdf - \
  | grep -nE 'Relief Event|Compensation Event|Change in Law'

Finally, the schedules nobody reads

Handback requirements, insurance minimums, and the reporting regime. These are where disputes in year eighteen come from, which is exactly why they are drafted last and reviewed least.

  1. Payment mechanism and deductions.
  2. Termination compensation, all three cases.
  3. The five definitions that carry money.
  4. Handback, insurance, reporting.

Four passes, in that order. The document was never meant to be read front to back; no one who drafted it did either.
